Korea's briefing to its own food exporters is a live map of the compliance terrain Mamee's regional expansion must now cross
The Korea Agro-Fisheries & Food Trade Corporation, working with Korea's Ministry of Agriculture, held an online briefing on Wednesday for roughly 200 Korean food exporters on tightening non-tariff barriers in Indonesia and Vietnam — two of the region's biggest food import markets. Sessions covered Indonesia's mandatory halal certification and Indonesian National Standards (SNI) requirements, alongside a fresh round of revisions to Vietnam's food safety laws, with speakers urging exporters to tighten raw-material, manufacturing and labelling controls end-to-end rather than treat registration as a box-ticking step. An aT director summed it up bluntly: the regulations exporters need to prepare for "are rapidly increasing" in both markets.
Mamee angle: The briefing was built for Korean exporters, but it's an unusually clear preview of the paperwork terrain facing any SEA snack player pushing further into Indonesia and Vietnam — including Mamee's own regional distribution ambitions. Worth an internal audit of halal certification and SNI documentation now, while it's a filing exercise rather than a shipment stuck at customs.
