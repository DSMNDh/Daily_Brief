MALAYSIA CONSUMER VALUE
Malaysians would rather cook than pay the fuel surcharge on delivery
A new Rakuten Insight survey — the July edition of its Malaysia Cost Pressure Pulse (MCPP) — found 82% of 1,003 Malaysian respondents would change their behaviour if food-delivery or ride-hailing prices keep rising on the back of fuel costs, and only 8% say they would simply absorb the increase. Cooking at home and switching to public transport top the list of substitutes consumers reach for first. Researcher Ming Yao Kong told Malay Mail the resistance is sharpest when a price hike is "just a bill you're being asked to help cover" rather than a visible service upgrade.
Mamee angle: every household that swaps a delivery order for cooking at home is a pantry-snacking and instant-noodle occasion up for grabs — value multipacks and home-meal companion snacking (Mamee Noodles, Mister Potato) sit exactly where this substitution behaviour lands, and it's a stronger value hook than competing on delivery-app promos Mamee doesn't control.
