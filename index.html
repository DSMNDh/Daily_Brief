Malaysia has rewritten the Food Act for the first time since 1983 — and packaging, online listings and penalties all moved at once
The Dewan Rakyat has passed the Food (Amendment) Bill 2026, the first comprehensive review of the Food Act 1983, hours before the chamber adjourned for today's Budget 2027 tabling. Three changes land directly on packaged snacks: new controls on food contact materials — the packaging itself — formal monitoring of online food sales, and penalties rising to RM25,000 or five years' imprisonment, against the RM1,500 to RM3,000 fines Health Minister Dzulkefly Ahmad cited from past fatal food-poisoning cases. The ministry has already submitted 23,553 requests to block social media accounts and e-commerce listings between 2023 and September 2026, which it described as an increasingly worrying trend of non-compliance. The Director-General of Health also gains power to close premises and publish information on dangerous products without waiting for a court order, and pesticide residue non-compliance in local vegetables has climbed from 9% in 2023 to 14.1% in the first half of 2026. Enforcement will be risk-based, with a grace period and phased education for industry.
Mamee angle: Two exposures open at once. Food-contact-material documentation becomes a gating item on every new pack format rather than a back-office formality, and every TikTok Shop listing, Shopee bundle and creator-led live sale now sits inside a named enforcement regime with a specific blocking mechanism behind it. Use the grace period: audit creator scripts and listing claims now, while advice is still the remedy rather than a RM25,000 compound.
