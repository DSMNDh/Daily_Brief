Regulatory Watch
Malaysia tightens the gate on imported food — HACCP and GMP become the price of entry
Malaysia's Ministry of Health has published guidelines implementing a new Imported Food Safety Assurance Plan (IFSAP), gazetted as Part IIB of the Food Regulations 2026 (Amendment No. 3) — the guidelines landed on 8 September. From 1 March 2027, no imported food may be sold in Malaysia unless it comes from a premises with an FSQD-recognised food safety assurance plan covering HACCP and GMP, replacing the country's old back-end model of port sampling and label checks with upfront, whole-chain certification of the exporting factory. The compliance runway is now under 18 months, and it lands just as retailers report shoppers trading down toward cheaper imported and private-label snack alternatives.
Mamee angle: every foreign snack brand competing for Malaysian shelf space now has a compliance clock running. Mamee's domestic HACCP/GMP base becomes a genuine cost and speed advantage over imported private-label and grey-market entrants — worth testing a "Made & Certified in Malaysia" value cue at shelf as trade-down accelerates.
