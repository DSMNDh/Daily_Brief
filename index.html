Market Watch
Oil surges to a six-week high as Brent tops $94, dragging Bursa Malaysia into a third losing session
Brent crude jumped 3.4% to close at $94.07 a barrel on 22 July — its highest level since 8 June — after an eleventh consecutive night of US strikes on Iranian military sites reignited fears of a Strait of Hormuz disruption, with Houthi threats in the Red Sea and an attack on a Black Sea pipeline terminal adding to supply anxiety. Malaysia's FBM KLCI slid 0.52% to 1,711.37 the same day, its third straight losing session, as banking and plantation counters led a broad regional risk-off move. The timing bites: crude palm oil is already trading at RM4,400–4,650 a tonne this month on a tightening supply outlook, so a sustained oil spike now pressures freight, resin packaging and biodiesel-linked feedstock costs across the snack supply chain at the same time.
Mamee angle: treat this as an early-warning window, not a surprise bill. Lock in packaging and freight rates now while competitors are still reacting to the headline, and hold pricing and promo discipline through August so any margin hit lands on the category, not just on us.
