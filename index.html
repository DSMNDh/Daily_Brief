Malaysia and China renew — and expand — their currency swap line to RM130bil, locking in FX terms through 2031
Bank Negara Malaysia and the People's Bank of China renewed their bilateral currency swap arrangement for a fifth time since it launched in 2009, effective for another five years and expanded from RMB180bil to RMB220bil (about RM130bil). The stated aim is to deepen monetary cooperation, expand local-currency settlement for bilateral trade and investment, and safeguard financial market stability between the two economies. China remains Malaysia's largest trading partner, and the facility now covers a materially larger buffer of ringgit-renminbi liquidity than at any point in its 17-year history.
Mamee angle: for an exporter selling into 100-plus countries and sourcing packaging, flavourings and raw materials with China-linked exposure, a bigger and longer-dated RMB-MYR swap line is a genuine hedge story — worth flagging to finance and supply-chain teams as a talking point for 2027 input-cost planning, and a quiet reassurance for trade partners who ask about currency risk.
